\section{Training-Time Sampling Laws}\label{app:training-time-laws}


The diffusion baseline draws a discrete denoising index uniformly,
$k\sim\operatorname{Uniform}\{0,\ldots,K-1\}$, where $K$ is the number of diffusion steps. This is not
a draw from the continuous transport time used by the flow-based models.

For instantaneous-velocity matching, let $U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1.5,1)$ and set the transport
time to $\tau=1-U$, on the axis where $\tau=0$ is noise and $\tau=1$ is data. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
  \tau\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,1.5),\qquad
  p(\tau)=1.5(1-\tau)^{1/2},\quad 0<\tau<1.
\end{equation}
Thus the implemented draw weights the noise end of this transport axis. The expression
$\operatorname{Beta}(1.5,1)$ in the training configuration describes $U$ \emph{before} the reflection,
not the time passed to the network.

Analytic and consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching each draw two independent standard
normal variables $Z_1,Z_2$ and transform them by
\begin{equation}
  T_i=\frac{1}{1+\exp[-(P\sidx{std}Z_i-P\sidx{mean})]},\qquad
  P\sidx{mean}=-0.4,\quad P\sidx{std}=1.0,\quad i\in\{1,2\}.
\end{equation}
On the same noise-at-zero axis, $\operatorname{logit}(T_i)$ therefore has mean $+0.4$ and standard
deviation $1.0$. The ordered pair is $r=\min(T_1,T_2)$ and $t=\max(T_1,T_2)$. For each example,
independently with probability $0.5$, the implementation then sets $r=t$, giving a zero-length interval and an
instantaneous-velocity anchor; otherwise the interval has length $t-r>0$. Before ordering, the
logit-normal density of each $T_i$ is
\begin{equation}
  p_T(u)=\frac{1}{u(1-u)\sqrt{2\pi}}
  \exp\!\left[-\frac{(\operatorname{logit}(u)-0.4)^2}{2}\right],\qquad 0<u<1.
\end{equation}
These laws are the samplers behind the entries in \autoref{tab:train}; the average-velocity pair and
the losses to which it is supplied are defined in \autoref{sec:method:engine}.

\section{UAV-corridor under Every Selection Rule}\label{app:uav-corridor-rules}
\longdata{four tables, every selection rule per constraint and projection method; the paragraph below
stays, the tables move to the web link}

\autoref{tab:uav-corridor} prints each model at each budget at its best selection rule under each
projection method. Where a cell's flights all traverse the corridor, the three rules of a flow-based
model lie within about two violating steps per flight ($2.1$ at most) and within about twenty control
steps ($21.4$ at most) of one another, cumulative projection cost leaving the fewest violating steps in
most cells. Under per-step projection over the hump at $\nfe = 1$ no rule of any flow-based model
traverses, and at $\nfe = 2$ only temporal consistency does, on one flight of ten for analytic
average-velocity matching, two for the consistency-interpolated model and none for
instantaneous-velocity matching; the zero violating steps of the other rules there belong to flights
that stall short of the apex until the step cap, not to constrained traversals. The baseline's rules
differ by about five violating steps under the tilt, $0.8$ under cumulative projection cost against
$6.1$ under temporal consistency, and its cumulative-cost rule over the hump traverses on nine flights
of ten.

\chapter{Compute Environment}\label{app:repro}\label{app:compute}

Every training and evaluation of this thesis ran on one node of the institute's cluster, scheduled by
SLURM; \autoref{tab:compute} lists it. The network runs on the GPU; the MuJoCo rollout and the SLSQP
projection run on the CPU, which a job shares with the other jobs of the node, as it shares the memory
and the I/O. The two MuJoCo MPC cells of UAV-s-curve (\autoref{sec:res:uav:controller}) ran in a
second environment on the same node, a copy of the first with MuJoCo 3.x and MJX, in which the
controller's predictive simulations run on the GPU beside the network. Each job log records the git
revision, the node and the GPU it ran on.
