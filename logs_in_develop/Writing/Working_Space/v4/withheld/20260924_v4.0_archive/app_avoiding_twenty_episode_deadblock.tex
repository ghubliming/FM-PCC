\section{Twenty-Episode Evaluation on D3IL-avoiding}\label{app:avoiding-twenty-episode}

The extended evaluation uses twenty episodes per training seed and geometry, one hundred per geometry.
Coverage at $\nfe=20$ is listed in \autoref{tab:coverage-k20}.

\paragraph{The baseline reproduction at this protocol.}
\autoref{tab:target} reproduces \ac{DPCC} at the protocol \ac{DPCC} itself reports on, and that is the
reproduction the thesis uses. The same configuration was also run at twenty episodes per seed.
\autoref{tab:target-twenty} keeps that result here rather than beside the published one, because it is not
a like-for-like reproduction: it finished the \emph{both-hard} geometry for one training seed rather than
five (\autoref{tab:coverage-k20}), so the three geometries it averages are not equally covered. It is
recorded and it is not relied on --- no claim in \autoref{ch:results} or
\autoref{sec:setup:baselines:avoiding} rests on it.

What the larger sample buys is resolution. At ten episodes per geometry a success rate resolves to
$0.1$, so a rate of $1.00$ there is not evidence of a rate of $1.00$: the baseline's perfect rates fall
to $0.95$ and $0.92$ at one hundred episodes, while its tightened configurations move by at most $0.08$
(\autoref{tab:tiers}).

At that resolution the selection rule separates the models more than the objective does
(\autoref{tab:state-headline}).

Analytic average-velocity matching is the model most sensitive to it. With temporal consistency it reaches
$0.993$ at one network evaluation, in $61.0$ control steps; with cumulative projection cost, the rule
\ac{DPCC} itself selects, the same model falls to $0.943$ and $72.0$ steps, and at two evaluations that rule
picks plans that stall, $98.0$ steps. Instantaneous-velocity matching is indifferent: every rule puts it
between $0.990$ and $1.000$ at one and two evaluations. The diffusion baseline does best with cumulative
projection cost, $0.983$ in $69.0$ steps.

Temporal consistency is therefore used for the flow-based operating points on this task. Under the
per-step projector the models differ, at their own best rule, by less than the gap in what one action
costs to compute.

The ordering is the one the protocol of \ac{DPCC} gives
(\autoref{tab:avoiding-dpcc-protocol}), and the two budgets do not change places: instantaneous-velocity
matching reads $0.997$, $0.997$ and $0.990$ at $\nfe=1$ against $0.960$, $0.970$ and $0.987$ at
$\nfe=20$ under the three rules, the largest gap between them being eleven episodes in three hundred.

\paragraph{Matched step budgets.}

\autoref{tab:avoiding-budget20} compares each flow-based model at two and twenty network evaluations
on the seed--geometry cells available at both budgets.

No model improves at twenty. Instantaneous-velocity matching goes from $1.000$ success with constraint
satisfaction to $0.970$, analytic average-velocity matching from $0.990$ to $0.955$, and
consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching from $1.000$ to $0.950$; the control-step column moves
by three steps in one direction for the first and by one to three in the other for the other two. Ten
times the network evaluations raise the time per control step for all three models.

The same ten-fold rise in the budget multiplies the time to
compute one action by $17$ for instantaneous-velocity matching and by about $36$ for both
average-velocity models, from under thirty milliseconds to between $0.48$ and $1.02$ seconds. At twenty
evaluations two of the three take longer per action than the diffusion baseline at the same budget.
