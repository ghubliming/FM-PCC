## The storyline rewrites (v3.90 + v3.78)

Checked sentence by sentence against the guide ("if a sentence dilutes one of these lines, the sentence is wrong")
and against v3.99's conclusions. No numbers in the abstract.

- **Abstract.** "instantaneous-velocity and consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching reach the reliability of
  DPCC" was the leveling pattern the guide names, and "endpoint projection reduces planning time in the evaluated
  manipulation comparisons" is not true as a rule. Now three sentences, one per step of the study: on obstacle
  avoidance the average-velocity models lead — CI-MeanFM dominates DPCC's diffusion model with its own network
  (same success within the constraints, fewer control steps, a small fraction of the computation), MeanFM gives up
  little of that success for the same saving, and no endpoint-projection configuration improves on them; on
  alignment MeanFM dominates again and endpoint projection is the better projector on the constraints at the budget
  the task needs; on the quadrotor projection carries over to a new plant and to three dimensions, simple generated
  demonstrations make FM enough, and the tracking controller decides whether a plan is flown. *MeanFM's
  one-episode-in-thirty is said as "gives up little", not hidden inside "dominate" (guide: honesty still holds).*
- **§1.4 item 4 (endpoint projection).** Now: no step to guide at one evaluation and no configuration improving on
  per-step projection there; where it guides, as reliable or more and cheaper for the two average-velocity models;
  on alignment more contexts free of violations in most comparisons at a comparable time; on UAV-corridor a trade.
  The FM $K=3$ exception is in the `\srcnote`.
- **§1.4 item 5 (alignment).** "moves the object closer" → dominance, as Ch 6 states it: closer to the target than
  the other objectives and the baseline, more contexts free of violations, a fraction of the baseline's time.
- **§1.4 item 6 (quadrotor).** The result sentence now covers all three scenes as Ch 6 concludes them (pillars:
  declared constraints kept, collisions outside them end flights; corridor: projection carries over to three
  dimensions, objectives indistinguishable on straight-line data; s-curve: the controller decides, the cascaded
  geometric controller is the better one).
- **Left as they were:** items 1–3 state the dominance axes already (item 2 carries MeanFM's small loss), and the
  rest of Ch 1–4.
