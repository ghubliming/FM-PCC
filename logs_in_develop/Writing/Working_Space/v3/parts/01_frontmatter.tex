\ifstandalone
Diffusion planners capture multimodal demonstrations, and Diffusion Predictive Control with
Constraints (DPCC) enforces constraints by projecting denoising iterates onto the feasible set.
However, their long denoising sequence is fixed during training and repeated at every control step.
This thesis replaces diffusion with deterministic transport along an ordinary differential equation.
Three objectives are studied: instantaneous-velocity matching, analytic average-velocity matching and
consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching, whose target mixes the instantaneous velocity with
the network's own prediction; the integration budget is selected at deployment. On the
obstacle-avoidance benchmark of DPCC, the aim is to preserve success and
constraint satisfaction while reducing network evaluations, the time to compute one action and the
control steps required to reach the goal. Constraint enforcement forms a second contribution. DPCC projects the
current iterate; endpoint projection predicts and projects the final trajectory,
then guides later sampling steps towards it. The analysis identifies when the budget and activation threshold permit guidance. After evaluation on the DPCC benchmark, the controller is extended to two
more complex settings built for this thesis: vision-conditioned object alignment and control of an
underactuated, open-loop unstable quadrotor. On obstacle avoidance, instantaneous-velocity and
consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching reach the reliability of DPCC with fewer control
steps and less computation. Endpoint projection reduces planning time in the evaluated manipulation
comparisons, while analytic average-velocity matching improves visual alignment.
