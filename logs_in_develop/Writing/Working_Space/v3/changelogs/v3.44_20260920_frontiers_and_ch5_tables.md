## 0 · 🆕 Cost frontiers for D3IL-aligning and UAV-corridor

**The author asked where the Pareto plot is for the other two environments.** `fig:avoiding-tradeoff`
existed only for D3IL-avoiding. Two more are built and in the draft.

They are **not** the same construction, and the difference is forced by the data rather than chosen.
On D3IL-avoiding every eligible configuration solves the task, so the frontier is **cost against cost**:
control steps against time per control step, at quality held within a band. On the other two:

* nothing on D3IL-aligning succeeds or fails — what a model delivers is a **distance**;
* on UAV-corridor a flight that passes the goal keeps flying to the **episode limit**, so its control-step
  count measures the limit, not the arrival.

On both, therefore, the frontier is **outcome against cost**, and the caption says so.

## 3 · The optimiser `\guard` becomes main text

The `\guard` under Table 5.8 said the settings are not uniform. It is now two paragraphs of ordinary
prose, in the chapter's own voice, saying **why**: the four objectives fit different quantities — a noise
residual, an instantaneous velocity, an average velocity over an interval — so the scale and the variance
of the gradient differ by construction, and each model was given the batch, step size and weight
averaging under which **its own loss curve converged**. The second paragraph states what is held fixed
and therefore what the comparison rests on.

## 4 · Table 5.9 stops enumerating and starts explaining

* **Activation threshold** no longer lists $\eta$ per budget; the cell says what is evaluated.
* **Episode limit** is re-worded as what it is: a stop.
* Three new paragraphs after the table. What an **episode** is, start to end, and that the limit is a hard
  cap on wall-clock time — a planner that has lost the task would replan forever, and every quadrotor
  control step costs a projection solve. Why the threshold is **lowered** as the budget rises: at
  $\nfe=20$ with the default $\eta=0.5$, ten programs per candidate plan per control step for up to $400$
  steps is not affordable, and what endpoint projection needs is a fixed *number* of guiding steps, not a
  fixed *share* — so the two alignment budgets are compared at three guiding steps each rather than at
  half the sampling steps each. And the one move in the other direction, $\eta=1.0$ at the avoiding
  budget floor, is the same argument at the opposite end. Why the quadrotor's tightening adds $0.31$\,m.

## 8 · 🔍 The low-budget rows were challenged and checked

**The author's question: where does the "magic" $\nfe=2$ data come from, when $\nfe=20$ was supposed to
be better?** It was recomputed and it stands. Two independent checks:

* the corpus of record (`batch_avoiding_combined_20260919_132703`, via `avoiding_rules_by_protocol.py`)
  reproduces every printed cell of `tab:avoiding-dpcc-protocol` exactly;
* an **earlier, separately produced batch** (`temp/2508/batch_avoiding_combined_20260825_143212`) gives
  the same ordering: FM at $\nfe=1,2,5$ at or above $\nfe=20$.

So the draft now says this in the open rather than leaving it to be doubted. A new three-paragraph block
in §6.1.3.1 states that a cell at DPCC's protocol is **30 episodes**, so $1.000$ means thirty of thirty
and the two FM extremes are four episodes apart; that the **extended** protocol, ten times the episodes,
still puts $\nfe=1$ at least level with $\nfe=20$ (largest gap: eleven episodes in three hundred); and
that this is **not** evidence that fewer solver steps make a better plan — the plans do get tighter with
the budget (§6.1.1) and on D3IL-aligning the outcome improves with it throughout
(`fig:aligning-tradeoff`). This benchmark is **saturated**: one evaluation already yields a plan the
projector can make feasible, and the other nineteen cost about $28\times$ the time per control step for
nothing the score can register.
