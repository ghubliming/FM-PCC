## H0. The s-curve loss of control: is it a PID parameter problem?

**Checked against the prior analysis** (`Gen15/U11/DA_20260911_corridor_ball_first_results.md` §7,
`Campaign_20260907_five_missions/DA_20260909_T5_mjpc_vs_pid_s_curve.md` §3) and the controller source
(`uav_naive_test/flight_controller.py:61-70`). **Probability that tuning the reachable PID parameters
fixes the scene: low.** Probability that the *controller configuration* is the cause: high. They are not
the same statement, and the difference is the answer:

* The failure is **attitude-domain** — `divergence_aborted` fires with `inverted` (body z · world z < 0)
  on all ten flights at step 395–421, at altitude.
* The three gain presets that exist (`pid_default`, `pid_high_gain`, `pid_low_gain`) scale **only**
  `Kp_pos` and `Kd_pos`, by $\pm20\,\%$. `Kp_att`, `Kp_omega` and the thrust clip are untouched by all
  three, so **by construction they cannot address a loss of attitude**. Eval also hardcodes
  `pid_default` (`eval_mix_uav.py:1482`), so the gains are not reachable from a config at all.
* The mechanism the data names is the **velocity setpoint**, not the gains: brake-to-rest has the
  *lowest* tracking error on the scene (0.29–0.34 m, the best in the table) and never advances, while
  MuJoCo MPC tracks *worse* (0.42–0.49) and arrives 3/3. Tight tracking plus no progress is a
  standstill, not a tracking failure.
* The cheap untried experiment: **`UAV_MIX_CONTROLLER=pid_const_v` on s-curve.** One env var, no code
  change, already validated. Confirmed from `per_rollout_detail.csv` that it has **never been run on
  this scene** — it exists only on pillars. That is the experiment that would test the setpoint
  hypothesis directly.

**Recommendation: keep the status quo claims, and no re-run is needed for them.** The chapter already
says the limit is the tracking controller and claims no model ordering from the scene, and even MJPC
converts "never arrives" into "arrives, still violates" — S&C stays 0.000 either way. Two sentences were
added rather than changed: §6.3.4 now states that what separates the two controllers is the setpoint and
not the gains, and the `\guard` on `tab:uav-controller` records that the gains were never tuned and that
the available presets cannot reach the attitude loop — so the section claims a setpoint limit, not that
no tuning of this controller could fly the scene.

## H3. The error this uncovered, and fixed: `fig:k-ladder`'s mechanism

Chasing H2 showed the same confusion sitting in the draft. The diffusion series of `fig:k-ladder` is
`AVOIDING_T1_DIFFUSION_FOLDERS` = `H8_K1_…`, `H8_K10_…`, `H8_K20_…` — **three separately trained
checkpoints**, one per budget. The draft described that curve as "a diffusion model evaluated away from
the discretisation used in training", which is a different experiment from the one that produced it.

Rewritten in three places (§6.1.1.4 prose, its following paragraph, the figure caption): each diffusion
point is its own model, moving along that curve costs a training run, and the flow curve is one checkpoint
per model read at every budget. **The claim the figure supports does not weaken — it sharpens.** For a
flow-based model the step budget is a dial that can be turned after training; for the diffusion baseline
it is a training decision a later choice cannot undo. The PENDING note asks the DA to check that no other
table reads a diffusion budget as an inference-time choice.
