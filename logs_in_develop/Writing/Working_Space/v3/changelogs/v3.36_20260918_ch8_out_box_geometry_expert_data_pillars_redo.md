## 2. The alignment context is random, and the box pose is not observed

v3 §5.2.2 said only "each episode starts from an initial box pose, called a context". Two facts that
matter for how hard the task is were nowhere in the draft:

* the context is **drawn per episode** — box in $x\in[0.4,0.6]$, $y\in[-0.25,-0.10]$, yaw $\pm90^{\circ}$;
  target in $x\in[0.4,0.6]$, $y\in[0.20,0.35]$, yaw $\pm90^{\circ}$ (`gym_aligning/envs/aligning.py:62--67`).
  Obstacle avoidance starts every episode from one fixed pose with fixed posts, so a policy there can
  learn a route; here two episodes are two different pushes;
* the observation is **$(\vect{p}\sidx{des},\vect{p})\in\R^{6}$ and nothing else** — no box pose, no
  target pose. Verified as the dataset actually trained on:
  `ParityAligningDataset.OBS_DIM = 6` (the 9D visual trajectory), selected at
  `train_visual_aligning_dpcc.py:168--170`. The 23D `StateOnlyAligningDataset`, which *does* carry box
  and target pose, is used for no result in this thesis. So the two camera streams are the sole source
  of the box's position.

Both are now a paragraph in §5.2.2. Chapter 4 implies the first and contradicts itself on the
constraint set; sent to v2 as
[`FROM_v3_20260918_aligning_observation_and_constraints.md`](../../cross_draft/to_v2/FROM_v3_20260918_aligning_observation_and_constraints.md).

## 5. UAV-pillars recomputed from the batch CSV — the result is narrower than it was

The old §6.3.1.2 reported **one budget** and ranked the models by **one mean over eleven
configurations of two different kinds**. Recomputed with `analysis/pillars_grid.py`, reading
`candidates_per_variant.csv` cell by cell and resolving every candidate's model through
`candidate_axes.csv`:

**The data is sound.** Every number the old table printed matches the CSV, and the models are
architecture-matched: `mf` and `af` are `bbunet`, and `fm`'s folder `…FlowMatchingODE_9D` carries no
backbone suffix because the quadrotor flow-matching model predates that convention — it is the U-Net.
This is not the `Folder_Name` trap of v3.32.

**The reading was not.** Splitting the eleven configurations into the two blocks they belong to:

| | per-step, 7 configs, $\nfe=5$ | endpoint, 4 configs, $\nfe=5$ | per-step, 7 configs, $\nfe=2$ |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| MeanFM | **0.586** | 0.700 | **0.429** |
| FM | **0.586** | **0.950** | 0.343 |
| CI-MeanFM | 0.386 | 0.325 | 0.300 |
| Diffusion ($\nfe=20$) | 0.080 | not evaluated | --- |

So on the configurations the two leading models share, they are **exactly level** — each ahead in
three of the seven cells, one level. The whole of instantaneous-velocity matching's lead is the
endpoint block, and at $\nfe=2$ the order reverses. And the scene's premise matters: **the unprojected
plan is already collision-free on all ten flights** for instantaneous-velocity matching and on nine for
analytic average-velocity matching, so what the projected rows rank is how much of an already feasible
plan each method keeps, not whether it can repair an infeasible one.

The section now opens with that, reports the two blocks as **`tab:uav-pillars` (every evaluated budget,
per-step)** and **`tab:uav-pillars-endpoint`**, and states the narrower claim. The budgets that exist on
this geometry are MeanFM 2 and 5, CI-MeanFM 1, 2 and 5, FM 2 and 5, diffusion 20; **MeanFM and FM at
$\nfe=1$ do not exist here** and are a `\hole` (they exist only on the earlier `pillars` geometry, a
different constraint set that cannot be pooled). The step-budget paragraph now carries only what the
table does not — the time cost, $69$--$79$\,ms at $\nfe=2$ against $0.9$--$1.7$\,s at $\nfe=5$ with
per-step projection. `tab:uav-pillars-best`, the flight-by-flight paragraph and `fig:uav-pillars-paths`
were already consistent with the recomputation and are unchanged.

Downstream: the UAV conclusion and `tab:summary-models` no longer quote the mixed-block mean.
