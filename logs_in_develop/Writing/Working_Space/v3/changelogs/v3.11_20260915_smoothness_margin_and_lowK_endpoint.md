## 3. §6.1.3 rebuilt as the smoothness argument, with a number behind it

Retitled **"Smoothness of the Plans before Projection"**. The section previously showed the figure and
the violation counts without saying why an unprojected comparison is needed at all.

The argument now runs: every reported number is measured after the projection and the tracking
controller, both of which smooth what the model produced, so the comparison measures the tolerance of
the control stack as much as the quality of the plan.

🆕 **That tolerance now has a number, and it is the baseline authors' own.** `\textcite{romer2025diffusion}`
Table 2 varies the sampling time assumed by the dynamics model inside the projection against the true
one, and reports success with constraint satisfaction of $0.99$ / $0.95$ at a factor of two in either
direction and $0.86$ / $0.77$ at a factor of four. **A dynamics model wrong by a factor of four still
satisfies the constraints in three quarters of episodes.** The margin the stack absorbs is far larger
than the difference between two generative models — which is exactly why the plans have to be looked at
directly, and why the raw-plan comparison is not decoration.

Read off the PDF this pass (`aux_repo/PAPERS/in Proposual/DPCC.pdf` p. 10) and re-verified after
writing. ⚠️ Stated accurately in both the chapter and the note: Table 2 varies the **projection's**
dynamics model, not the controller; it bounds the closed loop as a whole.

Also corrected the reading of the figure: the smoothness of the *executed* path is the controller's, not
the model's — which is the point of showing the plans instead.

The owed measurement is unchanged and now says what it would settle: until a jerk / path-length /

## 4. Endpoint projection across the step budget — the K ladder, including $\nfe=10$

**Yes, the $\nfe=10$ evaluation exists**: `DA_20260824_does_HF_pay_when_it_actually_runs.md`, batch
`temp/1108/Revised_2/batch_avoiding_combined_20260811_221322`, MeanFlow U-Net, $\eta = 0.5$, five
training seeds × three geometries = 15 episodes per cell, at $\nfe \in \{1, 2, 5, 10\}$. It was not in
the draft. It is now `tab:hf-ladder` in §6.1.4:

| $\nfe$ | guiding steps | untightened | tightened |
| --: | --: | --- | --- |
| 1 | **0** | ahead of 2 of 3 | ahead of 3 of 3 |
| 2 | **0** | ahead of 2 of 3 | ahead of 0 of 3 |
| 5 | 2 | ahead of 2 of 3 | ahead of 2 of 3, level with 1 |
| 10 | 4 | ahead of 3 of 3 | ahead of 0 of 3 |

With the accompanying facts, all of them measured rather than argued:

- **Zero guiding steps at $\nfe=1$ and $\nfe=2$** — the evaluation records one program solved per plan at
  both, three at $\nfe=5$ and five at $\nfe=10$, matching the predicted count exactly.
- **At $\nfe=2$ with equal candidate counts the two methods produced bit-identical rollouts, at 2.45×
  the cost.** A degenerate cell is the same algorithm at a higher price.
- Endpoint projection's one systematic advantage is control steps, and the mechanism is not the
  projection: with four candidate plans the cumulative-projection-cost rule sometimes selects a stalled
  97-step plan, and endpoint projection, given one candidate, cannot make that selection. Said plainly
  in the text rather than left as a win.
- `\guard`: on this ladder endpoint projection ran with one candidate plan and per-step projection with
  four, so rows where it leads understate it and rows where it trails are confounded; the wall-clock
  columns are unusable for the same reason. The cost figure quoted is the equal-candidate one — **1.86
  to 3.57× per-step projection, never lower.**

## 5. The headline the author asked for

Stated in three places, because it is the shape of the whole section:

- **§6.1 opening** — the section reaches one operating point and stays there: MeanFlow reaches $0.993$
  at a single network evaluation, the smallest budget a model can be run at, and neither a second nor a
  larger one improves it. Two consequences follow, and the section is organised around them: the
  remaining differences between the models cannot be read off the success rates, and endpoint projection
  has no step to act on at this budget.
- **§6.1.4 close** — the two methods answer different questions. Endpoint projection becomes available
  only by raising the budget, which is the cost the flow-based models were adopted to avoid.
- **§6.4.2 and RQ3** — the condition is what limits the method on obstacle avoidance; the environments
  where it leads are the ones whose budgets are large enough for it to run.
