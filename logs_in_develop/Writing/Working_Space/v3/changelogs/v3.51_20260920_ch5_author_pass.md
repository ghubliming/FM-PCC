## 6 · §5.5.2 states how large the D3IL policies are

New paragraph after the reported success rates. On this task the heads are transformers of **four
layers at width 72** (BESO, DDPM-GPT) and encoder–decoder transformers of **two and four layers at
width 64** (DDPM-ACT, VAE-ACT) — D3IL Table 9, Aligning column — each **well under a million
parameters**. What carries the weight from images is the encoder: **each of the two camera streams has
its own ResNet-18 trunk, ≈11 M parameters**. This thesis takes that encoder over unchanged
(`sec:method:backbone` already says so) and puts the **4.0 M** temporal U-Net behind it — so the two
set-ups differ in the planner, not in how the images are seen. `tab:va-vs-d3il`'s Network row now
carries both sizes.

- Sourced from the paper **and** the repo: `aux_repo/d3il/configs/aligning_vision_config.yaml`
  (`n_layer: 4`, `n_head: 4`, `n_embd: 72`), `configs/agents/*_vision_agent.yaml`
  (`rgb_model` → `model_getter.get_resnet` → robomimic `VisualCore`, backbone `ResNet18Conv`,
  `share_rgb_model: False`, one trunk per view).
- **D3IL publishes no parameter counts.** The `\srcnote` says so, and says the bound on the heads
  follows from the layer and width values — not a number lifted from a table that does not exist.
