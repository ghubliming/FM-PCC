## 6 · 🔴 Mean $\pm$ spread, everywhere it belongs

**The author's finding, and it was right: a multi-episode average was being printed as a bare number.**

### What the spread is, and why that choice

New §5.4.4 `sec:setup:metrics:spread` states the convention once and every table points at it.

| kind of result | spread reported | why |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **multi-seed** (all D3IL-avoiding tables) | sample sd **across the five training seeds** | a claim there is about a *model*, not about one training run. It is also the larger of the two available spreads, so it is the conservative one. Each seed is averaged over its geometries first, then mean and sd over the seeds. |
| **single-seed** (seed-6 avoiding tables, D3IL-aligning, the quadrotor) | sample sd **across episodes / contexts / flights** | there is no seed dimension. Where a cell spans several geometries the within-geometry sds are pooled. |
| **counts** (`12/12`, `20/20`) and **medians** | none | a count is not an average; a median is reported with the number of contexts behind it. |

The section also states what a $\pm$ is *not*: never a confidence interval, never a test. The
no-$p$-values rule of this thesis is unchanged.

## 3 · 🔴 The removed `\provisional` was a **real flaw**, and it is now written up

*"The instantaneous-velocity models have not been re-evaluated at unit scale."* Removed from §5.6.3 as
instructed — and then checked, because the author asked whether it was real. **It is**, and it is the more
serious of the two known flaws:

```python
x = 0.5 * torch.randn(shape, device=device)     # the sampler
noise = torch.randn_like(x_start)               # the training step
```

The sampler inherited from \ac{DPCC} (`aux_repo/dpcc/diffuser/models/diffusion.py:168`) starts from
**half** unit scale. MeanFM and CI-MeanFM were written with $\sigma=1.0$ and say so in a comment; the
diffusion baseline is consistent because \ac{DPCC} uses the half draw at both ends. **The mismatch is
instantaneous-velocity matching's alone, and it is present in all three environments.**

Impact, assessed per environment in
[`../../data_status/FLAW_20260920_known_flaws.md`](../../data_status/FLAW_20260920_known_flaws.md) §B:

* **D3IL-avoiding — nearly none.** FM is at the ceiling there ($1.000$ under every rule at $\nfe\le2$);
  there is no headroom for a correct prior to occupy. The thesis's principal claim is not affected.
* **D3IL-aligning and the quadrotor — possibly a lot.** FM is the weakest flow model on both, and a prior
  at half scale concentrates the sample towards the centre of the learned distribution, which is the
  reported failure mode. So *"FM is weaker than the average-velocity objectives on those two tasks"* is,
  on those two tasks, a comparison between one model sampled from the wrong prior and two sampled from
  the right one.

A fix needs **no retraining** — it is one character in three files and a re-evaluation. The draft does not
currently carry a sentence saying any of this; adding one to §6.2 and §6.3 is a one-line change and the
author's call.

The action-weight flaw (§A of the same file) gained a section **6** recording the author's assessment that
its effect is **tiny**, with the evidence that supports it: on D3IL-avoiding four models under two
different weightings land within a few episodes of each other, the parameter scales one row of eight of
the plan, and it cannot interact with the three dials Chapter 6 actually varies. The file is renamed
`FLAW_20260920_known_flaws.md` and every link repointed.
