## 1. The bug: the draft was rendering PNGs that no longer matched their SVGs

**A figure in the store ships as `.svg` *and* `.png`, and LaTeX renders the PNG.** The builders write
only the SVG; the PNG companion is made separately with `svg/preview_png.py`. So when a builder changed
and only the SVG was rebuilt, the export copied a **fresh SVG next to a stale PNG**, the bundle reported
"30/30 render, 0 placeholder" — and the PDF showed the **old drawing**.

That is what happened to `fig:constraints-aligning`. v3.36 redrew the box to its real $0.10$\,m
footprint with the centre marked, and the SVG has been correct since. The PNG was three passes old, so
the page kept showing the fixed-size marker. The request was carried out; the result never reached the
paper. That is on me, and it was invisible from inside the pipeline.

**Thirteen figures were stale**, some since v3.33:

```
da/fig_avoiding_k_ladder      da/fig_avoiding_projector_cost   da/fig_raw_goal_reached_K1
env/fig_constraints_aligning  env/fig_constraints_avoiding     env/fig_env_avoiding
env/fig_expert_aligning       env/fig_platform_x2_dimensions   env/fig_scene_aligning
env/fig_scene_avoiding        env/fig_scene_uav_corridor       env/fig_scene_uav_pillars
env/fig_scene_uav_scurve
```

All thirteen regenerated at scale 3 and re-exported. **`fig:constraints-aligning` in `v3/figures/` now
carries the box and its target as real $0.10$\,m squares at their recorded yaw, each with its centre
crossed** — verified by opening the exported PNG, not by trusting the build.

**The guard.** `export_to_draft.py` now **refuses to export** when any referenced figure's PNG is older
than its SVG. It prints the offending pairs and the exact `preview_png.py` command for each, and exits
non-zero. A silent mismatch between what the builder draws and what the thesis prints cannot recur
through this path.

## 2. `fig:expert-uav`: the vehicle drawn to scale at each route's tightest moment

A path is a line and an obstacle is a line, so a line that stays outside another line looks safe. The
quadrotor is $0.62$\,m across and collides with its **body**, which is the entire reason the constraint
set is inflated at all.

Each route now carries the X2 at its tightest moment: the four rotor disks and the body of
`fig:x2-dimensions`, inside the $0.31$\,m disk the projection inflates every obstacle by, red where the
body overlaps an obstacle and green where it clears. The point is chosen by **minimum clearance to the
obstacle surface**, not by violation depth, so a route that never violates still shows its closest pass
instead of defaulting to its first sample (`extract/expert_paths.py` now records `worst_xy`,
`worst_clearance` and `worst_overlap` per route).

It shows exactly what was asked for: on **UAV-corridor** the three bodies sit inside the slide, so the
violation is visible as overlap rather than inferred from a crossing; on **UAV-pillars** the bodies pass
between the pillar pairs with the gap visible; on **UAV-s-curve** the body clears the wall it runs
closest to. Caption updated to say that the vehicle collides with its body, not its centre line.

## 5. §6.3.1.2 — the recomputation is in the draft (answer, not a change)

The UAV-pillars section was rebuilt from the batch CSV in **v3.36** and is in the current draft and in
the last three bundles. Where to check it:

| what | where |
| :-- | :-- |
| the script | `Data_Analysis/DA_in_Paper/analysis/pillars_grid.py` — run it; it prints the grid, the head-to-head and the best configuration per model |
| the section | `chapters/06_results.tex`, `\subsubsection{UAV-pillars}` — opens "the scene on which the projection has the least to repair" |
| the tables | `tab:uav-pillars` (per-step, **every evaluated budget**) and `tab:uav-pillars-endpoint` (endpoint, $\nfe=5$) |
| the changelog | `changelogs/v3.36_…_pillars_redo.md` §5 |

What it found, in one line: the numbers were right and the models are architecture-matched (no
backbone mix-up — `fm`'s folder carries no `bb` suffix because the quadrotor flow-matching model
predates that convention, and it is the U-Net), but **one mean over two different kinds of configuration
hid the result**. Over the seven per-step configurations at $\nfe=5$, MeanFM and FM are level at
$0.586$; FM's lead is entirely the endpoint block ($0.950$ against $0.700$), which acts on a plan already
collision-free on all ten flights before projection; at $\nfe=2$ the order reverses ($0.429$ against
$0.343$). MeanFM and FM at $\nfe=1$ do not exist on this geometry and are marked as a hole.

If the section still reads wrong, the disagreement is with the reading, not with the DA — and
`pillars_grid.py` prints everything it is built on.
