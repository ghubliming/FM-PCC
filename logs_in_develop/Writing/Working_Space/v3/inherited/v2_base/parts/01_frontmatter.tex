\ifstandalone
Diffusion models have become a popular choice for robot trajectory planning because they capture the
multimodal behaviour contained in human demonstrations. Diffusion Predictive Control with
Constraints (DPCC) makes them suitable for constrained control by projecting each denoising step
onto the set of feasible trajectories. However, a diffusion model produces a trajectory through a
long sequence of denoising steps whose number is fixed during training, and a receding-horizon
controller repeats this sequence at every control step. This thesis replaces the diffusion model in
DPCC with flow matching and MeanFlow, which generate trajectories by integrating an ordinary
differential equation and allow the number of integration steps to be chosen at deployment, down to
a single step. The projection, network architecture, training data and benchmark of DPCC are
retained. The approach is then carried to two settings built for this thesis: camera observations
through a learned visual encoding, and an underactuated quadrotor through a cascaded geometric
controller. The thesis further compares
projecting the intermediate sample with projecting the final trajectory predicted from it, and
derives the step budget at which the latter becomes effective. Simulations of robot manipulation
tasks show that MeanFlow satisfies the constraints and reaches the goal as reliably as DPCC at a
fraction of its computational cost, and aligns objects with their targets more precisely when
planning from camera images.
\fi
