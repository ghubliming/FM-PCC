\ifstandalone
A generative planner pays for every plan it produces. Diffusion Predictive Control couples a
denoising planner to a per-step projection that enforces dynamics and safety constraints, and its
step budget is fixed when the model is trained. This thesis replaces that engine with deterministic
ODE transport --- flow matching and its average-velocity variants --- holding the backbone, the
projector, the data and the evaluation harness fixed. The step budget becomes a solver argument,
chosen at deployment.

The same stack is then carried across two changes. First the observation: a vision-conditioned
instance of the manipulation benchmark, in which two camera views are compressed to a fixed-width
latent and the generative backbone never sees an image. That latent is also what makes a few-step
engine usable here at all, because the average-velocity objective differentiates through its own
network. Second the embodiment: from a position-controlled arm to an underactuated quadrotor. The
planner emits a position increment, the increment accumulates into a setpoint, and a cascaded
geometric tracking controller turns the setpoint into four rotor commands. Written this way the two
robots are one control structure, one layer apart.

Across all three settings the system is measured against the diffusion baseline under Pareto
dominance at equal success and equal constraint satisfaction. \hole{Headline numbers --- write last.
Keep the abstract under $\approx 300$ words.}

Three results are methodological: a computable budget below which a guided sampler silently
degenerates to sample-then-project; a regime split between the two constraint arms, explained by
which point each hands to an identical projection operator; and two retained negatives --- an engine
that does not improve on its predecessor, and a geometry defect this work's own instrumentation
found.
\fi
