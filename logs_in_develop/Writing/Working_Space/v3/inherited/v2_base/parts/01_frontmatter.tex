\ifstandalone
Diffusion planners capture multimodal demonstrations, and Diffusion Predictive Control with
Constraints (DPCC) enforces constraints by projecting every denoising iterate onto the feasible set.
However, their long denoising sequence is fixed during training and repeated at every control step.
This thesis replaces diffusion with deterministic transport along an ordinary differential equation.
Three objectives are studied: instantaneous-velocity matching, analytic average-velocity matching and
consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching, whose target mixes the instantaneous velocity with
the network's own prediction; the integration budget is selected at deployment. On the
obstacle-avoidance benchmark of DPCC, the aim is to preserve success and
constraint satisfaction while reducing network evaluations, wall-clock time and the control steps
required to reach the goal. Constraint enforcement forms a second contribution. DPCC projects the
current iterate at every sampling step; endpoint projection predicts and projects the final trajectory,
then guides later sampling steps towards it. The analysis identifies the step budget at which this
guidance becomes active. After evaluation on the DPCC benchmark, the controller is extended to two
more complex settings built for this thesis: vision-conditioned object alignment and control of an
underactuated, open-loop unstable quadrotor. Simulations show that analytic average-velocity
matching is as reliable as DPCC with fewer control steps and at a fraction of its
computational cost; endpoint projection can reduce constraint-enforcement time, and
vision-conditioned planning can align objects more precisely.
\fi
