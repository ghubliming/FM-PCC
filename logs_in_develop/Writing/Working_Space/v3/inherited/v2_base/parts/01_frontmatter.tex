\ifstandalone
Predictive control with a generative planner has to pay for its samples. Diffusion
Predictive Control couples a denoising planner to a per-step projection that enforces
dynamics and safety constraints, and inherits diffusion's step budget: the number of
network evaluations is fixed at training time and cannot be reduced afterwards. This
thesis replaces that engine with deterministic ODE transport --- flow matching and its
few-step variants --- while holding the backbone, the projector, the data and the
evaluation harness fixed, so that the step budget becomes an inference-time choice.

Across a state-based manipulator benchmark, a vision-conditioned variant of it, and an
aerial embodiment built for this work, the resulting system is evaluated against the
diffusion baseline under a strict definition of improvement: Pareto dominance at equal
success and equal constraint satisfaction. \hole{Headline numbers -- write last, after
Results is settled and the powered runs land.}

The thesis makes three further contributions beyond the engine swap. First, a
correctness condition for evaluating in-loop guided samplers: a step budget below a
computable threshold reduces the guided sampler to sample-then-project, so that a
substantial class of published-style comparisons measures nothing. Second, a measured
regime split between the two constraint-enforcement arms, with a mechanism that explains
it: projecting a predicted clean endpoint is cheaper per solve than projecting an
off-manifold iterate, and the advantage appears exactly where the guided sampler becomes
well-defined. Third, negative results are retained rather than discarded --- an
additional engine that does not improve on its predecessor, and a benchmark defect
diagnosed by this work's own instrumentation.
\fi
