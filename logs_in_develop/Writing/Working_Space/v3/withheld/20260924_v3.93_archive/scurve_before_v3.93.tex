% ---- s-curve conclusion (v3.92) ----
UAV-s-curve takes the tracking controller onto an extreme trajectory, and three limits separate on it. The
first is the controller's: stability at the second turn. Most flights of the average-velocity models and of
the baseline invert there under the cascaded geometric controller, just past the crossover, $11.6$ to
$15.9$\,s into the flight, and on the selected plans MuJoCo MPC keeps every flight upright and brings every one
within $0.3$\,m of the goal point, at about twenty-four times the cascaded law's cost per control step ---
twelve times the cost of generating the plan, and some seven times what the operating point of D3IL-avoiding
spends on an entire action. The second is that this rescue depends on the plan: under per-step projection the
same controller loses every flight, seven of them inverted against the outer wall of the second straight,
where the cascaded geometric controller still brings five of ten across. The third limit is the plan's: every
commanded path cuts the second inside corner of the crossover, crossing the gap about $0.2$\,m later than the
demonstrations do and passing $8$ to $19$\,cm inside the corner's keep-out, and the cascaded geometric
controller follows it to within about a centimetre, so that no flight of any configuration, under either
controller, is free of violations. Neither remedy removes that cut as configured: MuJoCo MPC follows the
command less closely and violates more, and per-step projection, which binds the plan's measured positions as
\ac{DPCC} does rather than the commanded one, leaves the cut in place and costs four of the nine crossings.

The demonstrations are simple here too, one route of straight segments joined by arcs
(\autoref{tab:uav-demos}), and on them the average-velocity objectives are not ahead at the budget they are
built for: at one evaluation instantaneous-velocity matching crosses the finish line on nine flights of ten,
the consistency-interpolated model on six and analytic average-velocity matching on three, and for every
flow-based model the crossings fall as the budget grows. Since almost every flight lost is lost to
inversion, this order is read for the controller, not for the models alone.
\guard{Instantaneous-velocity matching is sampled from initial noise of half its training scale
(\autoref{sec:setup:protocol:avoiding}); that bears on its order against the average-velocity models, not on
the controller comparison, which flies the same plans under both controllers.}
