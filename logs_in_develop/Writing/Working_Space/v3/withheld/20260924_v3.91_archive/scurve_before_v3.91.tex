\provisional{Prefilled on the unprojected grid of \autoref{tab:uav-scurve} and the pilot of

\provisional{UAV-s-curve is where the tracking controller becomes the limit. On a route of two sharp turns
most flights end inverted under the cascaded geometric controller, $11.6$ to $15.9$\,s into the flight --- all
ten of analytic average-velocity matching at twenty evaluations and eight of ten of the baseline --- and the
flow-based flights follow their plans with a mean tracking error of $0.30$ to $0.33$\,m, more than twice the
$0.121$\,m by which the route clears the constraints, so that no flight is free of violations. The pilot places
the limit in the controller: plans that invert under the cascaded geometric controller arrive under MuJoCo
MPC, still violating, and the controller that flies the scene costs about $126$\,ms per control step against
$6$\,ms for the cascaded law --- more than generating the plan, and some seven times what the operating point
of D3IL-avoiding spends on an entire action.}

\provisional{The demonstrations are simple here too, one route of straight segments joined by arcs
(\autoref{tab:uav-demos}), and on them the average-velocity objectives are not ahead at the budget they are
built for: at one evaluation instantaneous-velocity matching crosses the finish line on nine flights of ten,
the consistency-interpolated model on six and analytic average-velocity matching on three, and for every
flow-based model the crossings fall as the budget grows. Since almost every flight lost is lost to
inversion, this order is read for the controller, not for the models alone.}
