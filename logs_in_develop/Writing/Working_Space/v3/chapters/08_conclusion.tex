\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary}\label{sec:conc:summary}

This thesis replaced the diffusion model of \ac{DPCC} with instantaneous-velocity matching, analytic
average-velocity matching and consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching, keeping the backbone,
the projection, the data and the evaluation code of the baseline. The
new models were first evaluated on the D3IL-avoiding benchmark of \ac{DPCC} with its setup
unchanged, where the published baseline result was reproduced, and then on a vision-conditioned
D3IL-aligning task and a quadrotor benchmark built for this thesis.

On the benchmark of \ac{DPCC}, the flow-based models hold their success with constraint satisfaction down
to a single network evaluation, where the diffusion baseline loses it, and they reach the level of the
baseline's own budget of twenty in fewer control steps and at about a thirtieth of the time one action
costs. Their plans reach the goal more often before any projection than the baseline's do at twenty
evaluations. On D3IL-aligning, analytic average-velocity matching ends closer to the target than
instantaneous-velocity matching and the diffusion baseline, and it is the only model whose outcome
improves as the budget grows. On UAV-corridor, analytic and consistency-interpolated average-velocity
matching pass the goal collision-free on every flight at three evaluations, at a sixth of the time of the
diffusion baseline, which stays collision-free but never reaches the finish line. UAV-s-curve yields no
supported combination: its limit is the tracking controller and not the plan. Of the two projection
methods, endpoint projection is selected only on D3IL-aligning, the one task run at a budget large enough
for it to guide sampling; the two benchmarks that operate at one and at three evaluations select the
per-step projection of \ac{DPCC}.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}\label{sec:conc:rqs}

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1 --- Generative model.] With the backbone, projection, data and evaluation code held fixed,
    analytic average-velocity matching is the model selected in each of the three environments that yield
    a result, alone on D3IL-aligning and jointly with consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching
    on D3IL-avoiding and UAV-corridor (\autoref{sec:res:summary:models}). It reaches that level at fewer
    solver steps and at less planning time per control step: on D3IL-avoiding it holds $0.993$ success
    with constraint satisfaction at one network evaluation, where the diffusion baseline reads $0.983$ at
    the twenty it was trained with, in fewer control steps and at about a thirtieth of the time one action
    costs (\autoref{sec:res:avoiding:conclusion}). Instantaneous-velocity matching clears the baseline on
    the same three axes at that budget and is behind the two average-velocity objectives on D3IL-aligning
    and UAV-corridor. The budget orders the three objectives only where the environment is hard enough to
    separate them: on D3IL-avoiding all three hold their success down to one evaluation
    (\autoref{sec:res:fewstep}), whereas on UAV-corridor the two average-velocity objectives fly every
    flight collision-free from three evaluations on and instantaneous-velocity matching does not reach
    that at any budget evaluated, and on D3IL-aligning analytic average-velocity matching improves with
    more evaluations while consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching does not.
  \item[RQ2 --- Constraints.] Endpoint projection is well-posed only with at least one guiding step
    (\autoref{sec:res:constraints:degenerate}), which is why the two environments that operate at one and
    at three network evaluations select the per-step projection of \ac{DPCC}
    (\autoref{sec:res:avoiding:conclusion}, \autoref{sec:res:uav:corridor}). Where the budget is large
    enough for it to act, on D3IL-aligning at twenty evaluations, endpoint projection is the selected
    method: it alone keeps all ten contexts free of violations, still moves the box in eight of them, and
    costs less per control step than per-step projection at the same budget
    (\autoref{sec:res:constraints}).
  \item[RQ3 --- Transfer.] The lead of analytic average-velocity matching carries over from state to
    camera observations and from the manipulator to the quadrotor; the step from instantaneous-velocity
    matching to diffusion does not carry over to camera observations, where the two are level. On
    UAV-s-curve the tracking controller, not the plan, limits the result
    (\autoref{sec:res:uav:controller}).
\end{description}
