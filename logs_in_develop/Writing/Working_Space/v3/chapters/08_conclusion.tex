\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary}\label{sec:conc:summary}

This thesis replaced the diffusion model of \ac{DPCC} with flow matching, MeanFlow and consistency
training, keeping the backbone, the projection, the data and the evaluation code of the baseline. The
new models were first evaluated on the obstacle-avoidance benchmark of \ac{DPCC} with its setup
unchanged, where the published baseline result was reproduced, and then on a vision-conditioned
alignment task and a quadrotor benchmark built for this thesis.

On the benchmark of \ac{DPCC}, MeanFlow at a single network evaluation reaches the success with
constraint satisfaction of the baseline in fewer control steps and at a small fraction of its
wall-clock time, and its plans are coherent before projection where those of the diffusion model at the
same budget are not. On alignment it brings the box closer to its target than the baseline and flow
matching in every context, and on the quadrotor it completes the corridor collision-free and leads on
the pillars. Consistency training is level with MeanFlow where the tasks cannot separate them and behind
it where they can. Endpoint projection takes less time than per-step projection in every environment;
its constraint satisfaction depends on the environment.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}\label{sec:conc:rqs}

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1 --- Generative model.] With the backbone, projection, data and evaluation code held fixed,
    MeanFlow is ahead of flow matching and of the diffusion baseline in every environment. Flow matching
    alone matches the baseline's success at lower cost. Consistency training is not ahead of MeanFlow in
    any environment (\autoref{sec:res:summary:models}).
  \item[RQ2 --- Budget.] The flow-based models keep their success down to one network evaluation on the
    obstacle-avoidance benchmark, while the diffusion model loses it below its training budget
    (\autoref{sec:res:fewstep}). On alignment MeanFlow improves with more evaluations and consistency
    training does not.
  \item[RQ3 --- Constraints.] Endpoint projection is well-posed only with at least one guiding step
    (\autoref{sec:res:constraints:degenerate}). With enough guiding steps it is the faster method in
    all environments; its constraint satisfaction is level with per-step projection on obstacle avoidance and alignment, higher on the pillars and lower on the corridor
    (\autoref{sec:res:constraints}). On obstacle avoidance that condition is not met at the budget the
    task is run at: with one or two network evaluations there is no guiding step, so per-step projection
    is the method there (\autoref{sec:res:avoiding:projection}).
  \item[RQ4 --- Transfer.] The order of the generative models carries over from state to camera
    observations and from the manipulator to the quadrotor. On the s-curve the tracking controller, not
    the plan, limits the result (\autoref{sec:res:uav:scurve}).
\end{description}
