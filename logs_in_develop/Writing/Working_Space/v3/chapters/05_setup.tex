\chapter{Experimental Setup}\label{ch:setup}

The evaluation has two phases. First, the generative models and projection methods are established on
the obstacle-avoidance benchmark of \ac{DPCC}, with its setup taken over unchanged so that the diffusion
baseline is met on its own benchmark. Second, they are tested further in two environments built for
this thesis: a vision-conditioned alignment task and a quadrotor benchmark with three scenes.
Throughout this chapter and the results, these environments are called \emph{D3IL-avoiding},
\emph{D3IL-aligning}, \emph{UAV-corridor}, \emph{UAV-pillars}, and \emph{UAV-s-curve}; the descriptive
subsection titles and these prefixed names refer to the same tasks. \autoref{sec:method:envs} describes how each environment is constructed; this
chapter gives, for each of the three, the task and constraint values, the data, the baseline, the
metrics and the evaluation protocol.

\section{Simulated Platforms}\label{sec:setup:platforms}
% =============================================================================

Every environment is simulated by the MuJoCo physics engine \parencite{todorov2012mujoco}. The two
manipulation tasks run the seven-joint Panda manipulator in MuJoCo, with the model files, task
definitions and demonstrations of D3IL \parencite{jia2024towards}. The quadrotor benchmark runs the Skydio X2 model from MuJoCo Menagerie
\parencite{zakka2022menagerie} with the quadrotor task patch distributed by MuJoCo MPC
\parencite{howell2022predictive}. MuJoCo is the simulator in every case; D3IL and MuJoCo MPC supply the
task files and data that run in it. \autoref{fig:platforms} shows both models on their own, and
\autoref{tab:platforms} gives the properties that enter the experiments.

\subsection{Obstacle Avoidance}\label{sec:setup:tasks:avoiding}

The obstacle-avoidance task is the benchmark of \ac{DPCC}, simulated in MuJoCo from the task files and
demonstrations of D3IL: the same environment, Panda manipulator, demonstrations, constraint geometries, projection configuration and evaluation
protocol \parencite{jia2024towards,romer2025diffusion} (\autoref{sec:method:env:avoiding}). Holding
that complete benchmark fixed provides the controlled validation of replacing DPCC's diffusion model
with the three flow-based objectives. A manipulator moves its
end effector across a field of six obstacles to a goal line (\autoref{fig:env-avoiding}). At test time
the plan is constrained by halfspaces that the demonstrations never respect, in three geometries --- \emph{top-left-hard}, \emph{top-right-hard} and \emph{both-hard}, defined in \autoref{tab:avoiding-geometries} and drawn in \autoref{fig:constraints-avoiding} --- together with the circular keep-out regions and the action box
of \autoref{sec:method:env:avoiding}. The geometries are those of the \ac{DPCC} configuration \parencite[Sec.~6.1]{romer2025diffusion}. Every
constraint also has a tightened version, shrunk by $0.025$ (\autoref{sec:method:dpcc}).
\srcnote{\texttt{config/projection\_eval.yaml}: \texttt{halfspace\_constraints['avoiding-d3il']},
\texttt{enlarge\_constraints: \{'avoiding': 0.025\}}; parity with \texttt{aux\_repo/dpcc} checked in
v2, \autoref{sec:method:env:avoiding}.}

\subsection{Vision-Conditioned Alignment}\label{sec:setup:tasks:aligning}

The alignment task of D3IL \parencite{jia2024towards} asks the manipulator to push a box into a target
pose on a table. The task runs in MuJoCo; its task files, demonstrations and camera set-up are D3IL's. Around them this thesis
built a constrained-control environment (\autoref{sec:method:env:aligning}): a three-dimensional plan
with its dynamics rows, a constraint set on the planned end-effector position and on the action, and
the coupling of the visual encoder to the generative network. The policy observes two RGB camera
streams, a front view and an in-hand view, instead of the state (\autoref{fig:aligning-cameras}). Each
episode starts from an initial box pose, called a context. The tightened constraints are shrunk by
$0.03$\,m.
\srcnote{\texttt{config/visual\_aligning\_eval.yaml}: \texttt{workspace\_bounds},
\texttt{action\_bounds: 'auto'}, \texttt{enlarge\_constraints: 0.03}.}

Obstacle avoidance is a planar task that imitation policies already solve: the best policies reported
by D3IL reach a success rate of $0.95$ on it \parencite[Table~3]{jia2024towards}, so it tests constraint
satisfaction on a plan that is easy to produce. This environment raises three things at once. The plan is
three-dimensional rather than planar; the policy sees the scene through two cameras and a visual encoder
rather than through a state vector; and the task itself is not solved --- the best image-based policy
reported by D3IL finishes roughly a third of its episodes (\autoref{sec:setup:baselines:aligning},
\autoref{tab:va-vs-d3il}). It therefore asks whether the \ac{DPCC} construction still holds where the plan
is harder to produce, and it puts the projection where only some episodes finish at all.

The active geometry, \emph{combined-5}, bounds the planned end-effector position by
$x\in[0.20,0.80]$\,m, $y\in[-0.45,0.45]$\,m and $z\in[0.02,0.50]$\,m. Its tightened counterpart moves
each face inward by $0.03$\,m, giving $x\in[0.23,0.77]$\,m, $y\in[-0.42,0.42]$\,m and
$z\in[0.05,0.47]$\,m.
\srcnote{\texttt{config/visual\_aligning\_eval.yaml}: \texttt{active\_geo\_variants: [combined\_5]},
\texttt{combined\_5.workspace\_bounds} and \texttt{enlarge\_constraints: 0.03}.}

\subsection{Quadrotor Benchmark}\label{sec:setup:tasks:uav}

The quadrotor benchmark was built for this thesis around the Skydio X2 model of MuJoCo Menagerie
\parencite{zakka2022menagerie} (\autoref{sec:method:env:uav}). The vehicle is underactuated and open-loop
unstable (\autoref{sec:bg:quadrotor}), and its plan is tracked by the controller of
\autoref{sec:method:deployment}. Three scenes are evaluated, in increasing difficulty
(\autoref{fig:env-uav}, \autoref{tab:uav-scenes}):

\begin{description}
  \item[UAV-corridor] --- the entry test. Two parallel walls leave a clear width of $1.90$\,m. A sloping
    halfspace, the slide, descends at $14^\circ$ from the upper wall at the entrance to $0.05$\,m below
    the centre line at the exit, and forces every route to descend by $0.25$--$0.49$\,m before the exit.
    The models are trained on the demonstrations of a narrower corridor and never see the slide, so it is
    a test-time constraint in the same sense as the halfspaces of D3IL-avoiding.
  \item[UAV-pillars] --- the main test. Six cylindrical pillars stand in two rows across three columns, and
    the vehicle must choose a way through them.
  \item[UAV-s-curve] --- the most difficult test. Two offset corridor segments, joined by a diagonal passage,
    force two sharp changes of direction.
\end{description}

these boundaries a further $0.025$\,m inward, the margin of \ac{DPCC}.

\section{Datasets}\label{sec:setup:data}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Obstacle Avoidance}\label{sec:setup:data:avoiding}

The demonstrations are those of the D3IL obstacle-avoidance task \parencite{jia2024towards}, as used by \ac{DPCC}. Each step
records the commanded and the measured planar end-effector position and the planar increment between
consecutive commanded positions (\autoref{sec:method:env:avoiding}). The dataset holds 96 demonstrations (\autoref{fig:env-avoiding}). None of them satisfies the constraints of top-left-hard, one those of top-right-hard and two those of both-hard (\autoref{tab:avoiding-geometries}), so satisfying the constraints is left to the projection.

\subsection{Vision-Conditioned Alignment}\label{sec:setup:data:aligning}

The demonstrations are those of the D3IL alignment task \parencite{jia2024towards}: its $900$ training
demonstrations, with the two camera streams rendered by MuJoCo. For this thesis each step is laid out as the nine-dimensional plan of
\autoref{sec:method:env:aligning} --- the three-dimensional increment and the commanded and measured
end-effector positions --- and paired with the two images. Cut into plans of eight steps, they give
$168\,274$ training windows.
\srcnote{Training logs of jobs \texttt{24407}, \texttt{24345}, \texttt{24124}, \texttt{25372}:
\texttt{[ ParityAligningDataset ] 900 episodes, 168274 windows (horizon=8, traj\_dim=9)}.}

In all three environments, a random tenth of the training windows is held out and used only to select
the checkpoint (\autoref{tab:train}).
\srcnote{\texttt{train\_test\_split: 0.9} in every resolved \texttt{Trainer} configuration.}

scene-specific parameters. The corridor with the slide reuses the demonstrations
and trained models of the corridor, because the slide is a test-time constraint.

\section{Baselines}\label{sec:setup:baselines}
% =============================================================================

Three generative models are compared with the diffusion model of \ac{DPCC} (\emph{Diffusion} in tables):
\emph{instantaneous-velocity matching} (\emph{FM}) \parencite{lipman2023flow}, which learns the
instantaneous velocity of the probability path; \emph{analytic average-velocity matching}
(\emph{MeanFM}) \parencite{geng2025meanflow}, which learns the average velocity over an interval through
an analytic Jacobian--vector-product target; and \emph{consistency-interpolated average-velocity
matching} (\emph{CI-MeanFM}) \parencite{zhang2025alphaflow}, whose consistency step ratio $\alpha$
interpolates the target between the instantaneous velocity at $\alpha=1$ and a stop-gradient prediction
towards the analytic average-velocity objective as $\alpha\to0$. In this thesis $\alpha$ is annealed from
$1$ and held at a final value
$\alpha_{\mathrm{end}}$, which each result states. Two projection methods are compared: the per-step projection of
\ac{DPCC} and endpoint projection \parencite{li2025hardflow}. All generative models share the temporal
U-Net backbone of the baseline, with $4.0$\,M parameters, unless a table states otherwise. Throughout
all figures and tables, the four models are labelled \emph{Diffusion}, \emph{FM}, \emph{MeanFM}, and
\emph{CI-MeanFM}, respectively; running prose retains the full mechanism names.

\subsection{Quadrotor Benchmark}\label{sec:setup:baselines:uav}

The quadrotor benchmark is new, and no learned planner has been evaluated on it before. Its closest
precedent is the quadrotor task distributed with MuJoCo MPC \parencite{howell2022predictive}, which flies
the same Skydio X2 model \parencite{zakka2022menagerie}. That task is described here as it is
distributed, because the benchmark of this thesis starts from its vehicle model and departs from it in
everything else.

\paragraph{The original task.} The scene is a racing course of eight square gates, each a frame of four
capsules with a $1.5$\,m opening, placed at different headings and heights around a closed loop. The
vehicle starts on the floor. A goal marker leads it around the course: it sits at one of twelve fixed
positions, between $0.75$ and $2.25$\,m above the floor, placed so that flying from one to the next passes
through a gate, and it advances to the next position whenever the vehicle comes within $0.5$\,m of it,
returning to the first after the last. The gates do not appear in the objective; passing through them is
a consequence of where the goal positions are placed.

The controller is Predictive Sampling, the sampling-based predictive controller of MuJoCo MPC, and the
simulator itself is its model. At each planning instant it perturbs its current control sequence into 32
candidate sequences of the four rotor thrusts, each parameterised by five spline points, rolls every
candidate forward in MuJoCo over a horizon of $0.5$\,s at a time step of $0.01$\,s, and keeps the
cheapest. The cost of a rollout sums four quadratic terms over the horizon: the distance of the vehicle
from the current goal position, weighted $25$; its linear velocity and its angular velocity, each
weighted $1.25$; and the deviation of each rotor thrust from the hover thrust, weighted $10^{-3}$. The
first control of the kept sequence is applied to the rotors, and planning repeats. Nothing is learned,
no demonstrations are used, and no success rate is defined: the task is an interactive demonstration of
the controller.

\paragraph{This benchmark.} The benchmark of this thesis keeps the vehicle model and the simulator and
changes the three things that make the task a test of constrained generative planning. The plan comes
from a network trained on demonstrations instead of from sampling against the true dynamics; the
environment is a set of walls, pillars and a test-time slide that the plan must not violate, instead of
gates that a goal sequence leads through; and the plan is a sequence of positions tracked by the
cascaded geometric controller (\autoref{sec:method:deployment}), instead of rotor thrusts applied
directly. Each flight is scored for success and for constraint satisfaction (\autoref{sec:setup:metrics:uav}).

\srcnote{MuJoCo MPC, \texttt{mjpc/tasks/quadrotor/task.xml} (\texttt{agent\_planner 0} = Sampling,
\texttt{agent\_horizon 0.5}, \texttt{agent\_timestep 0.01}, \texttt{sampling\_trajectories 32},
\texttt{sampling\_spline\_points 5}, \texttt{sampling\_exploration 0.3}, cost weights Position 25, Lin.~Vel.~1.25, Ang.~Vel.~1.25, Control
$10^{-3}$; twelve goal keyframes at heights $0.75$--$2.25$\,m) and \texttt{quadrotor.cc} (four residual terms, none on the gates;
goal advances within $0.5$\,m and wraps after \texttt{model->nkey}); \texttt{gates.xml}: eight gates of four capsules,
$1.5$\,m opening; X2 start \texttt{pos="0 0 0.1"} in \texttt{quadrotor\_modified.xml}. \texttt{task\_transition} lists thirteen

\section{Metrics}\label{sec:setup:metrics}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Obstacle Avoidance}\label{sec:setup:metrics:avoiding}

The metrics are those of \ac{DPCC} \parencite[Sec.~6.1]{romer2025diffusion}: the number of control
steps to reach the goal, the success rate of reaching the goal, the success rate of reaching the goal
while satisfying the constraints at every step, and the number of control steps on which a constraint
is violated.

To these this thesis adds the time to compute one action, the quantity \ac{DPCC} states for its own
method \parencite[Sec.~6]{romer2025diffusion}. It is measured around the planner at every control step
and covers everything the planner does to produce the action that is executed: generating the $B$
candidate plans, projecting them onto the constraint set, and selecting one. The same measurement is
used in all three environments, and
\autoref{sec:setup:protocol:compute} gives the machine every configuration was measured on.

\subsection{Vision-Conditioned Alignment}\label{sec:setup:metrics:aligning}

Each episode is scored by
\begin{itemize}
  \item the final distance between the box and its target in the table plane, summarised over the
    contexts by its median and its minimum;
  \item the final orientation of the box relative to the target orientation;
  \item whether the box was moved at all;
  \item the number of control steps on which a constraint is violated;
  \item the wall-clock time per control step.
\end{itemize}
When generative models are compared, the distance is measured on plans executed without projection, so
that it reflects the generative model alone; when projection methods are compared, it is measured with
projection.
\srcnote{\texttt{per\_rollout\_detail.csv}: \texttt{context\_final\_xy\_dist},
\texttt{context\_final\_box\_angle\_deg} against \texttt{context\_target\_angle\_deg}, \texttt{frozen},
\texttt{n\_violations}, \texttt{avg\_time\_ms}.}

\subsection{Quadrotor Benchmark}\label{sec:setup:metrics:uav}

Each flight is scored by
\begin{itemize}
  \item whether the goal is passed: the vehicle crosses the finish line of its scene. This is the success
    criterion of D3IL-avoiding transferred to the air --- there an episode succeeds when the end effector
    crosses the goal line (\autoref{sec:setup:metrics:avoiding}), here when the vehicle crosses the line
    that closes its route;
  \item whether it is collision-free: no control step on which the vehicle, with its body radius of
    $0.31$\,m, violates a constraint;
  \item success with constraint satisfaction: the goal passed on a collision-free flight, as on
    D3IL-avoiding;
  \item the number of control steps, and the final distance to the goal point of the route, which
    describes where a flight that does not pass the goal ends;
  \item the time to compute one action (\autoref{sec:setup:metrics:avoiding}), split into generation and
    projection.
\end{itemize}

The evaluation resets the environment to a fixed, enumerated set of contexts, the initial box and
target poses, so every model and projection method starts from the same ten situations. The results use
training seed 6 and one episode per context, drawn from the training contexts of the D3IL task rather than
its held-out set. As on D3IL-avoiding, every configuration is evaluated under the three selection rules
of \ac{DPCC}, on untightened and on tightened constraints.

Because the contexts are shared, two configurations are compared context by context: for each context,
which of the two leaves the box closer to its target. A result is reported as the number of contexts in
which one configuration is ahead, together with the median final distance over the ten contexts. The
diffusion model was evaluated with three episodes per context; its value for a context is the median of
the three.

\subsection{Compute Environment}\label{sec:setup:protocol:compute}

All training and evaluation reported in this thesis ran on a single shared GPU node of the
institute's cluster, scheduled with SLURM. The node carries two 16-core AMD EPYC~7282 processors
(32~physical cores, 64~hardware threads) and eight NVIDIA RTX~A5000 accelerators with 24~GB of
device memory each. \textbf{Every job was allocated exactly one GPU}: no result in this thesis comes
from a distributed or multi-GPU run. The full hardware and software inventory, including package
versions, is given in \autoref{app:repro}, \autoref{tab:compute}.
