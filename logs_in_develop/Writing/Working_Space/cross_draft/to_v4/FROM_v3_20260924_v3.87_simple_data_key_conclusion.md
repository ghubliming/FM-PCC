## 1 · UAV-corridor: the author's KEY CONCLUSION (for Ch 8)

- **Claim (author):** "in the expert data very simple case the Model NOT matter, this is KEY CONCLUSION, our expert data for
  corridor just a line".
- **The data (Ch 5 `tab:uav-demos`, `fig:expert-uav`):** straight lines from x = −2.8 to 2.8 m, on three lanes
  y ∈ {−0.12, 0, 0.12} m, at an altitude drawn once per episode from [0.90, 1.30] m. From any observed position the
  demonstrations continue along a single path.
- **The evidence (Ch 6 `sec:res:uav:corridor:conclusion`, second paragraph):**
  - **Before projection, the four models cannot be told apart.** Every flight reaches the end in 242–251 steps, and the
    violating steps of any two models lie within about 1 sd.
  - **After projection, at a shared budget and projection method,** the three flow objectives agree on violating steps
    to within 1.6 per flight. The budget, by contrast, takes them from ~10 at K1 to 1–2 at K20 (tilt).
  - Steps to the line differ by at most 15 of ~250–300, and no objective is fastest at every budget.
  - **The budget and the projection method order the configurations, not the objective.**
- **The mechanism, one sentence in Ch 6, under a `\guard`:** the average-velocity objectives correct the curvature that
  comes from averaging many continuations (Ch 2 `sec:bg:fewstep`). One path per observation leaves little to average.
  The curvature is not measured: what the scene measures is the tie. This is Ch 7 material if you want to develop it.
- **Supersedes the "For Ch 8" line of the v3.85 note** ("the average-velocity models reach the corridor's finish line in
  fewer steps at their cheapest budgets"). Those step differences stand in the corridor's projection text, but they are
  within 15 steps, and the fastest objective alternates. The lead claim is now the tie.
- **⚠️ Do not write "unlike D3IL-avoiding".** D3IL-avoiding's own conclusion already says "among the flow-based models the
  step budget and the selection rule set the ordering, not the flow objective". Two things are specific to the corridor:
  - the tie holds before projection as well;
  - its data give one path per observation.

## 3 · UAV-pillars: the collisions — two things, both the quadrotor's own (Ch 6 after `tab:uav-pillars-geo`, and `sec:res:uav:pillars:conclusion`)

- **Same demonstrations, planner and projection; only the plant replaced.** Success within the constraints still falls
  from 0.967–1.000 to 0.600–0.700, and the difference is entirely collisions.
- **Why they happen:**
  - the five pillars outside the declared sets are protected by nothing;
  - the plans pass them as closely as the demonstrations passed the posts, at the rod's radius, and the map makes that
    radius the rotor reach;
  - on 50 of 51 collisions the commanded path itself is inside the reach (not tracking).
- **What a collision ends:** a quadrotor is held up by its rotors alone, and a rotor strike in most cases costs it
  attitude control. Unlike the manipulator on its fixed base, it does not survive the contact, so the flight ends at the
  first contact.
- The text makes **no claim about collisions on D3IL-avoiding** (author).
- **Ch 7 / real-world practice candidates:**
  - declare every obstacle as a constraint;
  - or keep a margin beyond the rotor reach.
  Both are already named as scene-design statements in `DA_20260923_pillars_v2_live.md` §3.4.
