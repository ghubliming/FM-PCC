\section{D3IL-avoiding at Twenty Episodes per Seed}\label{app:avoiding-twenty}

Analytic average-velocity matching and instantaneous-velocity matching were evaluated a second time at
the budgets they are operated at, $\nfe=1$ and $\nfe=2$, with twenty episodes per training seed and
geometry in place of two: one hundred episodes per geometry and three hundred per configuration.
Everything else is the configuration of \autoref{tab:avoiding-dpcc-protocol}: the same five trained
checkpoints per model, four candidate plans, tightened constraints, the per-step projection of \ac{DPCC}
and the three selection rules. The comparison is within each model, the same configuration at two and at
twenty episodes. \autoref{tab:app:twenty-projected} prints it under projection and
\autoref{tab:app:twenty-raw} with projection switched off.
These are the twenty-episode evaluations that are complete on all five training seeds and all three
geometries. Consistency-interpolated average-velocity matching and the diffusion baseline have no
twenty-episode evaluation that is complete in this sense, and at $\nfe=20$, where one control step takes
about half a second (\autoref{tab:avoiding-dpcc-protocol}), none was completed for any model.

\paragraph{Success with constraint satisfaction.}
The two protocols agree. The operating point, analytic average-velocity matching at $\nfe=1$ under temporal
consistency, reaches the goal within the constraints in $298$ of $300$ episodes at twenty episodes per seed
and in $29$ of $30$ at two, and at $\nfe=2$ the counts are the same. Instantaneous-velocity matching under
cumulative projection cost reaches $299$ of $300$ at $\nfe=1$ and $300$ of $300$ at $\nfe=2$, against
$30$ of $30$ at both. In all sixteen configurations of the two tables, the count at two episodes lies
inside the range of counts that $95\,\%$ of two-episode evaluations drawn from the twenty-episode one
return.

\paragraph{What twenty episodes resolve.}
At two episodes per seed the count moves in steps of one episode in thirty, and the three selection rules
of each model read within two episodes of each other. At twenty they separate.
Analytic average-velocity matching under temporal consistency keeps $298$ of $300$ at both budgets, against
$279$ under random selection and $283$ under cumulative projection cost at $\nfe=1$, and $280$ and $278$ at
$\nfe=2$: the rule it is operated with is also the one with the highest success with constraint
satisfaction. The three rules of instantaneous-velocity matching, all at $30$ of $30$ with two episodes,
read $299$, $299$ and $297$ of $300$ at $\nfe=1$ and $299$, $300$ and $295$ at $\nfe=2$. Drawn from
these twenty-episode evaluations, a two-episode evaluation returns $30$ of $30$ with probability $0.81$ for
the operating point and $0.58$ for instantaneous-velocity matching at $\nfe=2$ under temporal consistency. The spread over the five
seeds narrows: for the operating point, the standard deviation of the success rate with
constraint satisfaction falls from $0.075$ at two episodes to $0.015$ at twenty.

\paragraph{Control steps and time.}
The step counts behind \autoref{sec:res:ablations} are reproduced. Under cumulative projection cost
analytic average-velocity matching takes $72.0$ control steps at $\nfe=1$ and $98.0$ at $\nfe=2$, against
$72.4$ and $97.2$ at two episodes, and under temporal consistency $61.0$ and $60.4$, against $58.6$ and
$59.4$. Across the sixteen configurations the two protocols differ by at most $2.4$ control steps. The
time per control step of analytic average-velocity matching agrees to within $4\,\%$ under projection and
$7\,\%$ without it; that of instantaneous-velocity matching is $5$ to $13\,\%$ lower in the two-episode
evaluation. The best rule of each model is the same at both protocols except for instantaneous-velocity
matching at $\nfe=2$, whose three rules are level at two episodes and ordered cumulative projection cost,
random selection, temporal consistency at twenty.

\paragraph{Without projection.}
Both models reach the goal in $297$ to $299$ of $300$ episodes, against $30$ of $30$ at two episodes, and
success with constraint satisfaction stays low: $36$ of $300$ for analytic average-velocity matching at
both budgets and $15$ and $30$ of $300$ for instantaneous-velocity matching at $\nfe=1$ and $\nfe=2$,
against $1$ and $3$ of $30$.
