## Answers

### Q1 — Does H8+8 make HardFlow win, and replan-1 make the DPCC projector win?

**No. The replan cadence does not decide which arm wins. The horizon and the flow-step count K do.**

| configuration | K=1 | K=2 | K=5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| H8 / r1 | **DPCC** | **DPCC** | **DPCC** |
| H16 / r1 | **DPCC** | **DPCC** | **HardFlow** |
| H16 / r8 | **DPCC** | **DPCC** | **HardFlow** |

The pattern runs left-to-right (K), not top-to-bottom (cadence):

- **The DPCC projector wins at H=8 for every K**, and at H=16 for K=1 and K=2.
- **HardFlow wins only at H=16 with K=5.** It wins there under *both* cadences.
- Switching r1 → r8 divides every arm's cost by ~8 and leaves the ranking unchanged.

Winner is decided on: success-and-constraints first, then cost at matched candidate count (§1.2). At H16 both families reach S&C 1.000, so cost decides — and the cost comparison is only meaningful after correcting the 4-candidate vs 1-candidate asymmetry (§1.2).

### Q2 — For practical use, is H16/r1 or H8+8 better than the old H8/r1?

**H16/r1: no. H8+8: yes, if the controller can buffer an 8-step segment; no if it cannot.**

| | vs old H8/r1 | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **H16 / r1** | fewer steps, but 1.4–5.7× more expensive per env step *and* per tick | **reject** — dominated by H8/r1 on cost and by H16/r8 on cost |
| **H16 / r8** (H8+8) | 1.3–5.2× cheaper amortised, fewer steps, 1.6–6.0× worse per-tick latency | **adopt if buffering is available** |

Cheapest configuration with S&C at or above 0.99 in each deployment regime:

| requirement | configuration | S&C | steps | s / env step | s / plan (worst tick) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bounded per-tick latency | **H8 / r1**, `dpcc-t-tightened`, K=1 | 0.993 | 61.0 | 0.0181 | **0.0181** |
| can buffer 8 steps | **H16 / r8**, `dpcc-t-tightened`, K=2 | 1.000 | 56.0 | **0.0110** | 0.0880 |

H8+8 at K=2 buys 1.6× lower total compute and 5.0 fewer steps, and costs 4.9× higher worst-case tick latency. That is the whole trade.

> Both H16 rows rest on 6 episodes. The cost columns are near-deterministic; the S&C column is not (§5).

### 1.2 The cost column needs a batch correction before it can be read

**DPCC and `diffuser` arms ran `batch_size=4`. HardFlow arms ran `B=1`.** Recorded in `run_provenance.json` (`batch_size_dpcc_arms: 4`, `hf_batch_size: 1`); the H8 CSV carries the same split.

| 2 | 1 × 4 = **4** | 1 × 1 = **1** | **4 : 1** |
| 5 | 3 × 4 = **12** | 3 × 1 = **3** | **4 : 1** |

### 1.3 Why horizon flips it: the two solvers scale differently

| K | SLSQP per solve, H8 → H16 | IPOPT per solve, H8 → H16 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.00196 → 0.01658 = **8.5×** | 0.02172 → 0.04067 = **1.87×** |
| 2 | 0.00205 → 0.01517 = **7.4×** | 0.02193 → 0.04050 = **1.85×** |
| 5 | 0.01234 → 0.10228 = **8.3×** | 0.02048 → 0.03833 = **1.87×** |

**Doubling the horizon costs SLSQP ~8× per solve and IPOPT ~1.86× per solve, at every K.** HardFlow's in-loop IPOPT is near-linear in the decision-variable count; DPCC's post-hoc dense SLSQP is superlinear (≈ H³, consistent with a dense active-set QP re-factorised each iteration over `transition_dim × H` variables).

SLSQP starts far cheaper per solve and loses that lead as H grows. Per-solve parity:

| K | SLSQP ÷ IPOPT at H8 | at H16 | extrapolated parity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.09 | 0.41 | H ≈ **24** |
| 2 | 0.09 | 0.37 | H ≈ **26** |
| 5 | 0.60 | 2.67 | H ≈ **10** |

At K=5 the crossover has already happened by H=16. At K=1 and K=2 it has not, which is exactly the pattern in the Q1 table.

### 2.1 H16 / r1 — reject

Lengthening the horizon while still replanning every step does improve the trajectory: **2.9–35.3 fewer steps** in all six rows, and S&C reaches 1.000 everywhere (though the H8 reference is inside that interval at n=6, so the S&C move is not measurable).

It is rejected on cost. H16/r1 pays 1.4–5.7× more per env step than H8/r1, and — because it still replans every step — that is also its per-tick cost.

Going to r8 costs back part of the step gain (−4.7 to +6.7 steps against H16/r1, worse in 4 of 6 rows) but buys 6.3–7.6× lower amortised cost. Against H8/r1, H16/r8 still comes out ahead on steps in 4 of 6 rows (§2.2). That leaves H16/r1 with no regime where it is the right choice: strict per-tick budgets prefer H8/r1, buffered deployments prefer H16/r8.

## 4. Tightening is mandatory at r8

**Tightened arms hold.** Per-cell S&C (6 episodes), r1 → r8:

| K | `dpcc-r-t` | `dpcc-c-t` | `dpcc-t-t` | `hf-r-t` | `hf-c-t` | `hf-t-t` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.000→1.000 | 1.000→1.000 | 1.000→0.833 | 1.000→1.000 | 1.000→1.000 | 1.000→1.000 |
| 2 | 1.000→0.833 | 1.000→1.000 | 0.833→1.000 | 1.000→0.833 | 1.000→0.833 | 1.000→0.833 |
| 5 | 1.000→0.833 | 1.000→1.000 | 0.833→1.000 | 1.000→1.000 | 1.000→1.000 | 1.000→1.000 |

Every 0.833 is one episode of six in one scene, and two of them move upward (`dpcc-t-t` at K=2 and K=5). At n=6, 6/6 spans [0.541, 1.000] and 5/6 spans [0.359, 0.996]. The claim this supports is that r8 does not visibly break S&C — not that it preserves it.

`dpcc-c-tightened` is the only arm at 6/6 in every cell of both cadences.

**Untightened arms collapse.**

| K | arm | violations / episode, r1 → r8 | S&C, r1 → r8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `hardflow_new-{r,c,t}` | 0.50 → **11.17** | 0.500 → 0.333 |
| 2 | `hardflow_new-{r,c,t}` | 1.33 → **33.50** | 0.667 → 0.333 |
| 5 | `hardflow_new-{r,c,t}` | 2.00 → **10.17** | 0.667 → 0.333 |

Per scene at K=2: `both 4→45`, `top-left 0→54`, `top-right 0→2`. Increases of up to 25× are far outside n=6 noise.

**Mechanism.** Projection guarantees the constraint on the plan, at plan time. Under r1 the guarantee is refreshed every step and tracking error never accumulates. Under r8 the robot executes 8 steps open-loop and drifts; an untightened plan sits exactly on the constraint boundary, so any drift crosses it. Tightening supplies the margin that absorbs the drift.

`Tracking error` of the unguided `diffuser` arm (the only arm that logs it), per scene:

| K | r1 | r8 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.041 | **0.050** |
| 2 | 0.040 | **0.040/0.062** |
| 5 | 0.030 | **0.055/0.169** |

Drift grows with cadence, up to 5.6× at K=5.

> **Transferable result:** replan cadence and constraint tightening are coupled knobs. Executing 8 steps open-loop raises tracking error up to 5.6× and untightened violations up to 25×, while tightened projection is unaffected. HardFlow's H16/8+8 is affordable *because* the projection carries a margin.

## 5. What these numbers cannot support

1. **n=6 cannot resolve S&C.** Every H16 S&C figure is 6 episodes, one seed. 6/6 and 0.867 are the same measurement at that n. Cost figures are near-deterministic and do not share this problem.
2. **Amortised cost is not real-time cost.** Whether H8+8's gain is real depends on the buffering assumption (§2.2), which is a design decision this project has not made.
3. **`avg_time` prints to 3 decimals.** At r8 / K=1–2 the values are 0.0083–0.0147, so quantisation is ±3–6%. Only the K=5 r8 figures are precise; §1.2 uses r1 for this reason.
4. **Candidate counts differ (4 vs 1).** Corrected for on the cost axis in §1.2. **Not** corrected on the quality axis: DPCC's S&C and steps edge at H8 may be bought by the 4-candidate fan, and nothing measured here separates that.
5. **`replan_steps=8` was never swept.** 8 was chosen to match HardFlow. Where the untightened collapse begins is unmeasured.
6. **No H16 diffusion baseline.** The DPCC-diffusion reference is H8 only.
7. **One seed (6), one task (avoiding-d3il), one backbone (UNet).** Nothing here transfers to aligning, UAV, or the SiT/DiT backbones without a run.

## 6. Citable claims

**Defensible:**

> On avoiding-d3il, adopting HardFlow's planning structure (H=16, execute 8) reduces amortised planning cost by 6.3–7.6× against per-step replanning at the same horizon, and by up to 5.2× against the H8 per-step configuration, with no measurable loss in success-and-constraints for constraint-tightened projection. Per-plan solver cost is unchanged; the gain is a reduction in planning frequency, paid for with a 1.6–6.0× increase in worst-case per-tick latency.

> Per constrained solve, doubling the planning horizon from 8 to 16 costs SLSQP ~8× and IPOPT ~1.86×, consistently at K = 1, 2 and 5. HardFlow's in-loop IPOPT scales near-linearly in the decision-variable count; the DPCC projector's post-hoc dense SLSQP scales superlinearly. Under a hard latency budget, HardFlow is the arm that survives growing H.

> Replan cadence and constraint tightening are coupled. Executing 8 steps open-loop raises trajectory tracking error up to 5.6× and untightened violations up to 25×, while tightened projection absorbs the drift and is unaffected.

**Not defensible:**

- That H16/r8 dominates H8/r1. It trades worst-case latency for throughput, and S&C is unresolved at n=6.
- That r8 improves S&C.
- That HardFlow beats the DPCC projector by 7× on cost. That figure is ~4× candidate fan and ~1.7× solver; matched, it is 1.9–2.4×, at H16 / K=5 only. At H16 K=1/2 and at every H8 cell, the matched-batch DPCC projector is cheaper.

## 7. H8+8 + HardFlow as the sim2real candidate — conditional

**Premise:** HardFlow never justified H=16 / execute-8. The paper states it once, in an appendix sentence, with no ablation and no comparison to the DPCC setup it says it follows; the value arrives with the flow-guidance codebase it forked. Full forensics: `../H8+8_U10/RESEARCH_did_HardFlow_justify_H16_T8.md`. **If we adopt the structure, the justification has to be ours.** The chain below is that justification, and it either closes or it does not.

### Condition A — is H8+8 HardFlow's own best configuration?

**Holds at K=5, and survives the batch correction:** matched at a common candidate count, HardFlow at H16/K=5 is still 1.9–2.4× cheaper than the DPCC projector (§1.2). At K=1 and K=2 it does not hold in either direction — see the Q1 table.

### The chain closes on exactly one cell

**H16 / replan-8 / K=5 / `hardflow_new-*-tightened`** — S&C 1.000 (6/6), 58.0 steps, 0.0273 s per env step, 0.2187 s per plan. It is HardFlow's best configuration, it beats the DPCC projector at every configuration tested, and the cost advantage is not a batch artifact. **At K=1 and K=2 the chain breaks and the DPCC projector remains the better arm.**

### Why this cell is the sim2real candidate

1. **The constraint guarantee is per-sample, not per-cadence.** HardFlow's terminal safety property holds at the sampled trajectory (`h(x̂_N) ≤ 0`, exact at `t_N`); `T` appears in zero equations of the paper and is an environment-loop wrapper outside the algorithm (`RESEARCH_did_HardFlow_justify_H16_T8.md` §6.2). Nothing in the guarantee degrades at T=8 in principle.
2. **Cheapest real-time budget in the grid.** 0.0273 s amortised, 0.2187 s worst tick — against the DPCC projector at the same configuration: 1.34 s (cheapest arm) to 1.94 s (best arm) per tick.
3. **It scales the right way with horizon.** Real deployments want longer lookahead; per solve, doubling H costs IPOPT ~1.86× against SLSQP's ~8× (§1.3). The gap widens in HardFlow's favour as H grows.
4. **B=1 means no candidate fan.** One deterministic trajectory, projected — no stochastic candidate selection to certify or reproduce.
5. **Fewest steps in the grid** (58.0), i.e. shortest executed trajectories.

### The risk that decides it, and it is a sim2real risk specifically

**T=8 executes 8 steps open-loop under a least-squares-fitted linear dynamics model.** Model error compounds across the executed prefix — and sim2real *is* model error. §4 already measures this in simulation, where the model is nearly exact: tracking error rises up to 5.6× and untightened violations up to 25×. On hardware the mismatch is larger, so the drift is larger.

Three consequences:

- **Tightening is not optional.** Every untightened arm collapses at r8 (§4). Any sim2real deployment of this cell must carry a margin, and the margin has to be sized against the *real* mismatch, not the simulated one.
- **The margin and the cadence must be co-tuned.** They are coupled knobs (§4). A cadence sweep with tightening as the second axis (§8.3) is the minimum evidence needed before hardware.
- **Buffering must actually be available.** 0.2187 s per plan, then 8 env steps played from the buffer. If the platform cannot buffer, this cell is not deployable and H8/r1 is the fallback (§2.3).

### Status

**Candidate, not a conclusion.** The chain closes on n=6, one seed, one task, in simulation, at one K. What it justifies today is a targeted evidence programme, not a hardware commitment:

| gate | what it settles | run |
|---|---|---|
| n=6 → n=300 at K=5, H16/r8 | whether the S&C 1.000 and the step lead are real | §8.1 |
| `HFFM_BATCH=4` at H16/K=5 | whether the cost win is a batch artifact | §8.2 |
| cadence × tightening sweep | how much margin T=8 needs, and whether T=8 is the right T | §8.3 |
| dynamics-mismatch injection at r8 | the sim2real risk above, measured before hardware | **new — not yet planned** |

Until the last row exists, "H8+8 + HardFlow is our sim2real solution" is a hypothesis with one supporting cell.
